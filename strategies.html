🧮 Mathematical Reasoning

Turn unfamiliar maths into a sequence of manageable decisions.

Core routine: Read → identify → estimate → choose a method → solve → check.
1
Working backwards
Start from the final condition and reverse each operation.
2
Elimination
Remove answers that break a condition before doing full calculations.
3
Patterns and relationships
Compare changes between terms instead of calculating every value.
4
Fractions & percentages
Convert to a form that makes the comparison easiest.
5
Ratio & proportion
Find the unit relationship before scaling up or down.
6
Multi-step problems
Write the target first and separate the problem into smaller links.
7
Estimate and check
Use an approximate answer to catch impossible results.
8
Time-saving methods
Use mental maths, answer choices and structure when they are valid.

🧠 Thinking Skills

Translate every condition exactly. Never add information that was not given.

Core routine: Translate rules → track constraints → eliminate → test the remaining possibilities.
1
Must vs could be true
For “must”, test whether every valid case supports the statement.
2
If/then reasoning
Separate a condition from its consequence and avoid reversing it.
3
Syllogisms
Use only the information stated; do not assume the reverse is true.
4
Ordering & arrangements
Place the strongest restrictions first, then fill flexible positions.
5
Classification
Find the shared rule rather than relying on appearance alone.
6
Analogies
State the relationship in words before applying it to the new pair.
7
Spatial reasoning
Track one distinctive feature through rotations or reflections.
8
Test possibilities efficiently
Use the most restrictive condition first to reduce the search.

📖 Reading

Every answer needs to be supported by the passage and the exact wording of the question.

Core routine: Read for purpose → locate evidence → eliminate → choose the most complete answer.
1
Main idea
Ask what the whole passage is mainly communicating, not one paragraph.
2
Inference
Combine clues from the text; do not invent facts outside it.
3
Vocabulary in context
Replace the word with each option and test which meaning fits the sentence.
4
Author purpose
Consider what the writer is trying to achieve with the whole text.
5
Tone and mood
Use repeated word choices and details to identify the attitude or atmosphere.
6
Best answer
Reject answers that are partly correct but do not fully answer the question.

✍️ Writing

Use your 30 minutes deliberately: plan, draft, develop, then edit.

Core routine: Decode prompt → plan 3–5 minutes → draft with purpose → edit strategically.
1
Decode the prompt
Identify the task, audience, purpose and any required idea or setting.
2
Strong opening
Create immediate interest without wasting the first paragraph on background.
3
Character & setting
Reveal character through choices, actions, dialogue and specific sensory details.
4
Escalate the conflict
Make the problem grow so the climax feels earned.
5
Show, don’t tell
Use actions, reactions and imagery instead of simply naming emotions.
6
Precise vocabulary
Choose exact words; advanced vocabulary only helps when it is natural and accurate.
7
Sentence variety
Mix sentence lengths and structures to control rhythm and emphasis.
8
Edit strategically
Check prompt relevance, sentence control, spelling, punctuation and clarity.

⚡ Exam-speed strategies

Use speed to protect time for the questions that need deeper thinking.

Skip & return

If a question is consuming too much time, mark it mentally and return after easier questions.

Estimate first

A quick estimate can eliminate impossible options before detailed calculation.

Check conditions

Before submitting, ask: does my answer satisfy every condition in the question?
